CCNA Practice Labs is a networking-education platform I designed, built, deployed, and currently operate. It began as a way to turn my own CCNA study materials into something more practical than a collection of notes. The project developed into a structured learning environment that combines guided coursework, original Packet Tracer labs, topic guides, quizzes, exam preparation, progress tracking, and tools for identifying areas that require additional study.

The platform addresses a common problem with self-directed certification study: learners have access to an enormous amount of information, but often lack a system for applying it and measuring whether they understand it. CCNA Practice Labs organizes that material into a ten-week progression. Publicly available video instruction is linked as a learning resource, while the supporting notes, lab exercises, quizzes, explanations, topic guides, diagrams, and assessment tools are original materials created specifically for the platform. The goal is to move students from passive viewing into configuration, troubleshooting, testing, and review.

The current course includes 47 lessons, 31 lab scenarios delivered through 79 Packet Tracer files, 30 topic guides, 30 course quizzes, and a growing bank of exam questions organized around the six CCNA exam domains. The authenticated dashboard records student activity and assessment results, presents performance by domain, identifies stronger and weaker areas, and gives students a focused path for review. Administrative tools support user management, email communication, problem reporting, content review, and the staged release of new assessments.

The project is also a working example of full-stack development and systems administration. The public site is deployed through Cloudflare, while the authenticated application runs on a Linux VPS using Next.js, PostgreSQL, PM2, SSL-secured database connections, and a migration-based data model. I manage the Git workflow, application configuration, production deployments, database changes, analytics, email workflows, automated testing, and incident troubleshooting. That work has included diagnosing memory-constrained builds, CPU saturation, failed deployments, database configuration problems, authentication behavior, and production availability issues.

Artificial intelligence has been part of the development workflow, but not a substitute for technical ownership. I use it to accelerate research, implementation, testing, documentation, and content review. I remain responsible for defining requirements, evaluating generated work, correcting inaccurate output, diagnosing failures, validating networking content, and deciding what reaches production. The project has required coordination across networking, software development, infrastructure, education, interface design, and business operations rather than relying on any one tool or discipline.

Public registration opened in September 2026, moving CCNA Practice Labs from a private development project into an operating customer-facing service. Exams and additional assessment material are being released in stages while their accuracy, consistency, and difficulty are reviewed. The project now serves two purposes: providing an affordable and practical CCNA study environment, and demonstrating my ability to learn unfamiliar systems, integrate them into a larger product, and maintain that product in production.
